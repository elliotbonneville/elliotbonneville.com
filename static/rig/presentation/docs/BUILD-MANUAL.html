Building the rack together

Preparation edition — September 13, 2026. Read one session at a time. The final power/startup map is still open; receiving and dry-fit checks are specified in BUILD-TODO.md. This manual does not authorize powering an incomplete installation. It makes the established frame work explicit while those decisions are finished.

Start with INVENTORY.md. Keep the working computer intact until the receiving frames and transfer plan are ready. Set the rack in its final location before loading it; do not plan to wheel a populated open rig around.

1. Prepare the workspace and label the parts

Use a clear, well-lit table beside the rack, a separate electronics handling area, small labeled containers and somewhere safe for each removed component. Keep drinks, loose screws and cutting work out of the electronics area. Use manufacturer packaging for storage; an antistatic bag is packaging, not a powered bench surface. Cover parts elsewhere while cutting pads or drilling.

Use TOOLS.md for the missing-tool checklist. Stage a metric ruler/tape, square, straightedge, caliper, spirit level, matching setup blocks, small clamps and a light. For fasteners, stage metric hex keys including 2, 3 and 4 mm, a Phillips screwdriver and small nut drivers/wrenches. M3 plain nuts are 5.5 mm across flats; M5 nuts are 8 mm. Harwin's drawing gives 4.8 mm across the standoff flats; select a close-fitting tool rather than rounding them with pliers. A small adjustable wrench is useful for holding a standoff during an unpowered trial. Follow FASTENERS.md: the included structural M5 pairs use the selected 6.0 N·m assembly target; shelf and PCB joints have separate instructions. Leave the CPU cooler block mounting untouched. Never use an electric impact driver on these parts.

Scissors, a cutting mat, removable labels and a pen complete the sorting kit. Drill, bits, deburring tool, eye protection and swarf cleanup belong to the shelf-hole fallback only. Their exact sizes depend on the selected attachment; keep that work separate from the computer.

2. Practice one extrusion joint

The screw passes through the bracket and threads into a nut inside the same face's slot. You turn the screw from outside; you do not hold a loose nut behind the entire extrusion. Only the later shelf-through attachments need an underside nut.

Practice with an empty extrusion and one HBLSS5-SEP kit. Insert the matching HNTP5-5 post-assembly spring nut through the accessible slot, following its manufacturer insertion orientation. It must sit below the slot lips. The spring holds its position while you align the bracket and start the M5×8 screw by hand. Leave it loose enough to slide for positioning. Check the bracket contacts both members squarely and the screw clamps it before running out of thread travel.

HNTF5-3 device nuts are a different, smaller part without the same spring holding feature. Pre-position them while the empty rail's working slot faces up. For the underside GPU clamp nuts, install and retain the hardware while you can still turn the empty frame over. Never invert the frame with GPUs in it.

Checkpoint: the bracket can be positioned when loose and held when snug; the nut stays captured below both lips. If it spins, lifts through the slot or the screw becomes tight while the bracket remains loose, remove it and inspect its orientation and screw stack. More force is not the fix.

3. Assemble the empty GPU frame

Use the original visualizer with GPUs and cables hidden, and the current rack view for orientation. Rack front is the side from which shelf measurements start. Cards run in a row from front to rear, and their exhaust points out the rack's left side.

  1. On the flat table, build two matching end frames. Each has two 220 mm posts, one lower 219.5 mm tie and one top 219.5 mm tie. Top tie surfaces finish flush with the post tops. Put one flat L plate on the outward face at each upper corner, with two screws in the post slot and two in the top tie slot. Use an angle at each lower tie/post corner. Keep these joints adjustable initially.
  2. Join the end frames with G-L1 and G-L2, the two 615 mm adapter rails. Their upper faces start 30 mm above the shelf datum: 10 mm lower tie clearance plus 20 mm profile. Their center spacing follows the two adapter mounting holes, nominally 100 mm. Four angles join these rails to the lower end ties.
  3. Add the 615 mm clamp and rear support rails, each with an angle at both ends. Their final heights follow the fully seated GPU; do not lock them to a ruler dimension before that trial. Keep the rear rail low enough not to lift a card during the first seating check.
  4. Add one shelf-foot angle at the bottom of each post, oriented inward along the row as shown in the shelf guide. Its vertical hole gets one kit screw and nut; its horizontal hole is reserved for a shelf bolt.
  5. Compare the two end frames, check their corners with a square, and compare both diagonals across the same outer rectangle. Check all four post bottoms sit on the table without rocking. Equal diagonals supplement the square; they do not excuse bent members or a twisted assembly.
  6. Set the two adapter rail tops in one plane with equal blocks and a straightedge at the front and rear. Work through opposite joints gradually, rechecking alignment after tightening. Use the 6.0 N·m target and dry-thread procedure in FASTENERS.md for the included M5 kit pairs only. Check clamp seating before loading; this is not a torque for PCB hardware.

The bare frame is nominally 655 mm long; plates/screw projections extend the modeled envelope to 671.4 mm. Do not mistake the 615 mm cut for the outside size. No diagonal braces belong to the current design. Lower ties start 10 mm above the shelf, while top ties are flush with the 220 mm posts.

Checkpoint: twelve members, sixteen angles including four feet, four flat plates; all feet touch and the adapter rails are coplanar. Confirm end screw access inside the rack before installing cards.

4. Fit the empty host platform

Use the host support view and HOST-MOUNT.md. On the bench, join the two 400 mm end beams to four 150 mm legs. The beam tops finish 170 mm above the shelf. Four angles join legs to beams; four more act as shelf feet.

Fit four 350 mm rails between the end beams, using eight angles. These angles sit in the open corner beside each rail, facing inward from the end beam; they do not sit inside the rail's aluminum. Leave the rails movable across the beams. The motherboard's actual holes will set their final positions.

Fit the two 125 mm retimer posts and their four angles from H-CARDS loosely. The upper angle of each post supports a card's metal bracket tab. Do not tighten that tab before the retimer is fully seated in the motherboard.

Checkpoint: twelve host members and twenty angles overall; 400 × 390 mm nominal footprint, platform top 170 mm above the shelf. All four legs touch; none falls into a vent or lands on an unsupported edge. Final motherboard standoff alignment happens with the disconnected board during transfer.

5. Trial shelf attachment before covering the fasteners

Use SHELF-FIT.md. Check U11 and U18 shelf separation and the underside clearance above the retimer posts. Start all shelf mounting hardware before tightening it; the shelves must be secured at matching heights on all four rack rails according to StarTech's instructions.

Place the empty frames. For each foot, verify the bracket and underside washer bear on sound steel, there is a tool path below, and the screw reaches through the locknut's locking section. Existing vents are preferred. Nominal coordinates are not drilling marks. If a foot cannot be supported and secured there, keep that interface open for the documented fallback rather than balancing it on a perforation edge.

The shelf stack is M5×16 screw → standard M5 washer → foot angle → shelf → 15 mm OD backing washer → M5 locknut. Do not attach electronics until every foot is securely retained and the shelf remains flat. Hold the nut with its wrench while turning the screw; locking resistance is normal after the screw enters the nylon section, unlike resistance while first starting the metal thread.

Fit switch supports and radiator feet while the area is accessible. The radiator must come off its stands for the hidden shelf bolts to be installed; fasten the empty stands first. Treat the eight switch-bottom and four radiator-shelf nuts using FASTENERS.md, with PCBs and radiator removed; allow the planned 24-hour cure and complete cleanup before loading these mounts. Then reattach the radiator. Follow RADIATOR-MOUNT.md.

PSU installation remains on hold pending replacement mounting. The drawing below reserves space for the two owned supplies; it is not an installation template. Do not mark shelf holes, cut the four reserved PSU pads, or apply threadlocker to the reserved fasteners from this drawing. The selected replacement must supply its own fit and fastening procedure before PSU installation and motherboard transfer. Keep fan and connector access clear in that procedure.

Apply the eight cable-cushioning strips on the right side of the GPU shelf as shown in MCIO-DRESSING.md. Rest loose cable bights on these pads and retain them with reusable wraps. Keep connector latches accessible and the shelf carrying cable weight.

Checkpoint: photographs above and below all anchors; no rocking, unsupported washers, fastener contact with a PCB, or unaccounted protruding screw. Complete all drilling, deburring and cleanup away from electronics before the transfer.

6. Trial one GPU and its powered slot adapter

Use one GPU bag first. Each adapter support is: M3 slot nut → two standard 0.5 mm washers → 30 mm standoff → adapter PCB → 0.5 mm washer → M3×6 screw. The base washers span the slot. The two standoffs must match in height and align with the adapter's two actual holes without pulling the PCB sideways.

Support the GPU independently while aligning its keyed edge connector with the adapter. A C-Payne switch is electrically connected by cables; the GPU does not plug directly into the switch or use it as a physical support.

GPU clamp stack and screw approach

After fully seating the GPU, bring its bracket clamp and rear padded rail to the card. The clamp uses its large central bracket opening: from below, M3×16 screw, standard washer, GPU bracket, standard washer, 9 mm spacer, standard washer, and the underside slot nut. The uppermost washer is necessary to span the slot. Pre-position the underside slot nut on the empty rail and lay out the remaining stack in order. The closed GPU bracket opening still requires the final screw to be inserted through the supported card; do not promise a fully preassembled clamp can simply slide over that opening. The exact tool path with a delivered card is a trial requirement; do not fill the row until that first clamp can be installed and released without levering on the card.

Raise the padded rear rail evenly until it contacts the intended solid shroud surface without lifting or twisting the GPU. It must not press a PCB, fan, connector or exposed component. Adhesive goes on the rail side of the pad.

Checkpoint: full connector seating remains unchanged as supports are snugged; no bowed board, lifted bracket or rocking support. Record actual clamp stack and adjustments, then repeat the proven method for the other seven positions. Use 75 mm nominal card centers and check the actual gaps remain open. Do not use a common rail to force mismatched cards into alignment.

7. Wiring and bring-up are separate sessions

Follow TRANSFER.md for the planned solo move, including the CPU cooler handling and remount requirements. Use the final labeled power map once POWER.md is resolved. Slot and main power for a GPU must belong to its assigned PSU. Use the numbered MCIO map and twenty cable labels for the data connections, with G1 at the rack rear and G8 at the front. MCIO pairs need correct lane ordering and independent strain relief. Keep enough service slack to reach latches; leave fan openings clear and avoid tight cable ties around connectors. No live insertion/removal is planned.

Before first power, account for every screw, reconnect and configure the complete CPU cooler, verify the initial test configuration, and review the connection photos. First establish host cooling and device detection. Add cards between complete shutdowns; simultaneous full load comes only after staged checks pass.

If something does not fit

Observation Next action
Screw resists immediately Back it out; check size, thread and alignment. Do not drive it through resistance.
Screw is tight but the part moves Check bottoming, missing washers and nut capture before changing length.
Frame rocks Unload, check the work surface and joint alignment; do not tighten it flat against a PCB or shelf.
GPU latch or connector will not release Support the card, inspect with a light and follow the actual latch; never pry with a screwdriver.
Standoff turns with a top screw Hold its flats with the correct tool while unpowered; recheck the base afterward.
Screw falls into equipment Stop. Disconnect power and recover it before continuing; do not shake a populated frame.
Hose/cable reaches only under tension Stop and revise the route or component position. Do not stretch it into place.
A washer overlaps another vent Treat bearing as unverified; move to a supported location or use the resolved fallback.
