Tightening and loosening prevention

September 13, 2026. Use this with the exact stacks in BUILD-MANUAL.md. The frame, shelf, PCB and cooler screws have different jobs. A torque setting for one must not be reused on the others. Work unpowered, with the part supported.

"Snug" here means that the mating surfaces have seated and the joint has no free movement. It does not mean tightening until the tool can no longer turn. For PCB screws, use the small tool with two fingers, not the leverage of a long key or ratchet. If a joint still moves after it is seated, investigate bottoming, misalignment or inadequate bearing instead of applying more force. The shelf fasteners deliberately have no invented numerical torque: the actual vent and thin-sheet bearing condition controls that assembly.

Basis for the frame's 6.0 N·m target

MISUMI's HNTP5 catalogue gives 6.8 N·m reference tightening torque for M5. Its bracket catalogue specifies CBM5-8 / HNTP5-5 for HBLSS5-SEP. The selected top-plate kits use the same screw/nut family. The bracket table's allowable-load figure is not a complete-frame moment or sway rating.

6.0 N·m is our engineering assembly target, not a separately published HBLSS5-SEP joint torque or a structural certification. With a ±6% tool, its 5.64–6.36 N·m range stays below the nut's published reference value. It gives a repeatable assembly setting with allowance for tool accuracy. Actual clamp seating, square joints, loaded deflection and loosening remain receiving/build checks. Do not use the nut reference as a reason to tighten a slipping joint harder or to apply 6 N·m to sheet-metal or M3 hardware.

Selected tool: Wiha 28655, 2–7 N·m, no adapter with Wiha 28549 straight 4 mm TorqueControl blade. The Wiha catalogue gives 0.25 N·m increments, ±6% accuracy and an 8 N·m blade rating. The blade inserts directly into the standard handle. Verify the received scale, setting tool and calibration certificate. Label the frame tool FRAME 6.0 N·m. No cooler-specific tool is required for this transfer.

Assemble and square each empty frame loosely, then tighten progressively around it. Keep the bit straight, turn smoothly and stop at the first torque release. Use an accessible orientation of the empty frame instead of an angled ball end for final tightening. If a nut spins or the bracket will not seat, remove and inspect it. After final adjustment, draw a small witness line across the bolt head and bracket; a moved line later signals inspection. The line itself does not lock the screw. Check all 78 installed kit screw/nut pairs, including the single-pair shelf feet and retimer-support angles.

Selected low-strength locking system for twelve metal-only M3 nuts

Use LOCTITE 222MS, 10 ml, 22221 / IDH 135333, with LOCTITE SF 7649 activator, 37509 / IDH 494147, for the plated metal interfaces. The 222MS data sheet supports small fasteners and disassembly with hand tools and calls for an activator on inactive metals. The SF 7649 data sheet permits brush or spray application, followed by complete solvent evaporation. This selection avoids adding a thicker locknut to the short switch studs.

Online sources: 22221 at Newark and 37509 at Pegasus. Neither supply is included in the 26 ordered mechanical lines, and ownership is unconfirmed. Check existing supplies before buying one container of each; see TOOLS.md. Its prices and cart receipts are historical references. The mounting procedure depends on having these supplies available.

  1. Dry-fit the empty stands and the switch standoffs, washers and nuts. Establish usable shelf bearing and adjustment before any adhesive is used. Mark positions.
  2. Remove the hardware for cleaning. Keep all electronics away. Clean mating metal threads using the selected IPA wipes, inspect for remaining oil/fibres and let them dry. Use ventilation and follow the container's handling instructions; protect eyes and skin.
  3. Apply activator to the mating threads away from the rig; let it evaporate fully. Never spray an assembled computer, its connectors, pads or plastics. Never mix liquid activator directly into the threadlocker bottle.
  4. Shake the 222MS bottle as instructed by Henkel. Apply only enough 222MS to wet the small nut-engagement region, then assemble immediately and snug the metal joint. Keep the bottle nozzle off metal and remove excess. Do not put adhesive into the female PCB standoff end.
  5. Plan a 24-hour undisturbed cure at normal room temperature before loading these mounts. This is a build allowance based on the data sheet's typical cure evaluation, not a promise for every plating/temperature. Make a spare nut/bolt sample alongside the assembly to confirm curing occurred; uncured material or an unexpected surface finish requires inspection before loading.
  6. Inspect bearing and add witness marks. Install electronics only after this work and cleanup are complete. If a treated joint is later loosened for adjustment, clean and reapply; do not count broken adhesive as a fresh lock.

Use hand tools for later removal and counter-hold standoff flats. Do not heat hardware beside electronics to overcome a stuck joint. Stop and remove the affected assembly for review if normal hand removal fails. Do not substitute medium/high-strength or wicking threadlocker in this small-fastener procedure.

Checks after assembly

Before installing expensive hardware, check that the empty anchored frame does not rock, the joints do not slide under gentle hand pressure and the rails stay square. Populate it in place; do not lift it by the GPUs or move a loaded rack. After the first controlled load cycle and again after the first day, shut down, disconnect power and inspect witness marks, supports and cable strain. A moved joint is a reason to unload and investigate, not to tighten a loaded PCB/frame.
