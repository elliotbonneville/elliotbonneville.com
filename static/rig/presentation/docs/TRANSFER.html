Disassembly and transfer plan

Revised September 9, 2026: the CPU cooler block stays attached. The receiving mounts, final power plan and tool/consumable list must be complete before this procedure starts. This plan assumes one person handling the hardware, with Codex reviewing checkpoint photos. It does not depend on having another person hold a radiator in midair.

The recorded case is a Meshify 3 XL. Use the case-specific access guide alongside this sequence: it identifies the removable top radiator carrier, PSU rear plate and concealed cable-cover screw from the factory manual.

T0 — Keep a route back to the known-working machine

Before shutdown, verify a current backup of important data and configuration. Record the installed drives, boot drive, network cables and working settings; do not reinstall the operating system, update firmware or uninstall the GPU driver as part of a mechanical move. Keep the case, original brackets and all original fasteners until the rack has passed commissioning.

Arrange downtime for anything using the computer. Finish the empty-frame trial, parts inventory and receiving-mount checks before ending service. Prepare a clear route between the case and rack and a protected resting place for every component. Do not carry a loaded case or shelf into a position you cannot reach and control comfortably.

Photograph the case interior, motherboard connections, GPU brackets/latches, radiator mounts and PSU cable labels. Label both ends of cables before removing them. Use labels such as OLD-GPU-1 and BOOT-SSD; avoid relying on remembered positions. Keep original case screws separate from the new M3/M5 hardware.

Checkpoint: recoverable data, known drive map, original configuration record, ready receiving frame, and no loose hardware mixed with components.

T1 — Shut down and disconnect

Shut down the OS normally and wait for shutdown to finish. Turn off and unplug all PSUs supplying the machine, including any secondary supply. Disconnect external cables that could snag during the move. Wait for fans to stop and standby indicators to extinguish. A stationary PSU fan alone does not prove power is disconnected. Never open a PSU enclosure.

Use appropriate static-conscious handling: avoid carpet/static-generating clothing, handle boards by their edges and use an ESD work surface/strap per its instructions if available. Never improvise a connection to a mains pin. Put each removed card in its original protective packaging or on the prepared ESD surface; do not stack bare cards or rest solder joints on metal.

Checkpoint: all power sources physically disconnected; case cool enough to handle; clean workspace and no cable pulling against the machine.

T2 — Remove the four existing GPUs one at a time

Begin with the extender-mounted card if it provides the clearest access and can be independently supported. Identify its bracket screws, any extra support, the power-connector latch, and the extender's slot latch before undoing anything. Use a light or phone close-up to inspect hidden latches while unpowered.

  1. Support the card's weight and unlatch its power connector. Grip the plastic connector body, not its wires. Remove display/other cables too.
  2. Keep supporting the card while removing its case bracket screws and any auxiliary restraint. Store those pieces in that card's original-case bag.
  3. Release the actual motherboard or extender slot latch. Do not guess its direction from a generic illustration; the model and view of the real latch set this step. Never reach in with a metal screwdriver to pry it open.
  4. Lift the card evenly out of the slot, without twisting its edge connector. If one end remains attached, set it back down and inspect the latch and bracket before continuing. Do not overcome resistance by rocking harder.
  5. Place it in its prepared storage and label it. Repeat for the next card with the clearest access. Remove the now-unused extender after its card.

Checkpoint after each card: intact connector/bracket, no unexplained force, all screws accounted for and remaining hardware stable. A blocked latch is a pause for a close-up review, not a reason to force the card out.

T3 — Support the connected radiator; leave the CPU cooler mounted

Do not loosen the cold-plate screws, CPU socket mechanism or hose fittings. Leave the CPU, RAM and installed M.2 drives in place. Label and disconnect the pump/fan electrical leads as needed; this does not open the sealed loop.

Prepare stable, padded radiator support beside the case and a receiving surface for the motherboard frame. Both must be within the actual relaxed hose reach at every stage. The radiator must stand securely without a hand holding it. Rehearse the support positions and board exit path before removing the last radiator-carrier or motherboard screw. Never pull the radiator around by its tubes.

Follow CASE-ACCESS.md to release only the radiator's case/carrier fasteners while its weight is supported. Set the radiator on its prepared support while the motherboard remains secured. Keep the fan assembly attached. If a case opening traps a hose or the radiator cannot rest securely beside the board's exit path, stop and revise the support/access arrangement. Removing the CPU cooler block is not a fallback in this plan.

Checkpoint: cooler block untouched; radiator stable; all hoses slack; board and radiator can be supported through the entire planned movement.

T4 — Transfer the motherboard with its cooler block attached

Preassemble and square the empty host frame. Disconnect remaining labeled motherboard leads and inspect for concealed front-panel, fan, USB and storage connections. Release the case screws with the board supported. Lift only by appropriate board edges, keeping the radiator stationary on its stable support. Do not lift by the cooler, RAM, heatsinks or socket, or let hose tension twist the board. Make short, supported moves between prepared landing positions; never carry the board and a loose radiator together in two hands.

Align all eleven supports in HOST-MOUNT.md to actual mounting holes. The board never rests on steel or loose extrusion. Adjust and tighten standoff bases with the board clear; return it to its protected landing surface as needed while keeping the connected radiator supported. Start all board screws by hand before gently seating them. Do not use a screw to pull the board sideways or down onto a mismatched support.

Checkpoint: all mounting holes supported, no PCB bow, cooler mounting undisturbed, radiator independently supported and hoses still relaxed.

T5 — Secure the still-connected radiator

With the board secured, move the radiator from its temporary support to the XSPC stands, supporting its body throughout. Reuse only the appropriate short radiator case screws; leave the fan screws in place. Check stand position and hose reach before fastening the radiator to the shelf. If the hoses cannot reach without tension, adjust the receiving arrangement; do not detach the block or force a fitting.

Reconnect using COOLER-WIRING.md: the 3-pin pump lead to CPU_FAN2/WP (pins 1–3, stable 12 V/full speed), and the supplied radiator-fan splitter to CHA_FAN3/WP with CPU as its temperature source. The latter is conditional on the real three fan labels and splitter fitting its 2 A rating; 0.38 A is the pump rating, not each fan's current. Verify lead reach and sensor mapping before the staged first-start procedure.

Checkpoint: undisturbed cooler mounting, positively mounted radiator, relaxed hoses, working pump/fan connections and no unaccounted screws. The first power-on still waits for the completed electrical review.

T6 — Transfer PSUs and fit the initial test configuration

Keep each PSU's modular cables with its own unit. For the cased supply, follow the rear-plate withdrawal sequence with a supported landing behind the case. Fit the resolved feet and positive restraints before connecting anything; never drill or open the PSU. Populate the initial retimer/switch/one-GPU configuration in the final bring-up plan. Use the documented support stacks and the labeled power/MCIO map. Install further cards only after that initial configuration passes its checks.

Pause here for the pre-power review. Photograph both ends of every initial power connection, the GPU's slot seating, the cooler connections and the area under the board. Record any departure from the planned stack or routing before turning on power.

Recovery if the transfer stops

Leave the hardware unpowered, supported and protected. A missing part or failed fit does not require putting everything back immediately. Preserve the labels and photographs so work can resume from a known checkpoint. If returning to the case is necessary, use the recorded original mounts and wiring while keeping the cooler block attached and radiator independently supported.

Sources: ASRock motherboard manual, SilverStone installation manual, original cooler specification. The case's actual latch access, solo handling clearances and final power map remain preparation dependencies. No current hardware has been disconnected.
