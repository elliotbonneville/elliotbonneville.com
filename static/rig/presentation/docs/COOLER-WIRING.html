CPU cooler wiring and first-start checks

Planning map, September 8, 2026. This uses the original SilverStone XE360-TR5 and ASRock WRX90 WS EVO. Keep the complete liquid loop. Final power release, fan-label current and lead reach remain prerequisites; this is not an instruction to power the current case or dismantle it now.

Preferred connection map

Label to put on cable Connection Configuration / electrical check
PUMP Radiator pump's 3-pin plug → CPU_FAN2/WP, motherboard layout No. 9 Align the keyed housing with pins 1–3; leave pin 4 unused. W_PUMP / full speed, stable 12 V. Original pump 0.38 A; header supports 2 A
RAD-FANS Three original 4-pin fan plugs → supplied three-way splitter D → CHA_FAN3/WP, layout No. 14 Combined maximum current, including any published startup current, must fit this 2 A header and the splitter. Initial full speed; later PWM control with Temp Source = Monitor CPU
CPU_FAN1 Unused in this map Do not mistake an unused header's zero reading for a stopped pump; identify each sensor by the cable label
PERIPHERAL ADAPTER C Retain with cooler accessories Not used when the pump is powered directly by CPU_FAN2/WP; do not connect two power sources to the pump

The supplied splitter keeps the assembly to two motherboard connections and avoids buying a fan hub. CHA_FAN3/WP offers more current capacity than the 1 A CPU_FAN1 header. ASRock explicitly permits using another header for radiator fans provided its temperature source is changed to CPU. This is a selected logical map, conditional on the actual three fan labels and splitter; the original SilverStone sheet lists pump current but does not establish each fan's current. Do not apply the pump's 0.38 A figure to the fans.

If the three fan currents exceed the header/splitter allowance, the map needs revision to separate headers or a rated powered hub before purchase release. Do not force this arrangement through by lowering fan speed: that does not establish adequate electrical capacity or startup behavior. Record fan labels and existing splitter condition without taking fans off the radiator just to read them. Check lead reach along the relaxed route before ordering extensions.

Identify the two headers

Read the PCB printing, not just the drawing's orientation. CPU_FAN2/WP is beside CPU_FAN1 and the board's power/reset area. CHA_FAN3/WP is by the same outer edge, near the 24-pin motherboard-power region. Their exact positions are shown on printed page 6 of the ASRock user manual. Neither is the nearby 3-pin ATX_CON1 secondary-PSU control connector or a 5 V addressable-lighting header. No jumper wires or guessed pin arrangements.

With power disconnected, align each housing's guide with the header's plastic key. Start squarely, supporting the board. If it needs unusual force, inspect orientation; do not push the pins sideways. Keep leads outside the fan blades, away from sharp shelf edges and clear of the motherboard support hardware.

Configure before the first CPU load

ASRock's November 10, 2025 guidelines, page 2 identify CPU_FAN2/WP as a pump connection with W_PUMP/full-speed defaults. An existing system may have changed settings; a documented default is not a check of this board's saved configuration.

  1. Record the existing fan settings as part of the transfer notes. On the assembled receiving rig, use its supported fan-control interface to confirm PUMP is full-speed/stable 12 V and RAD-FANS is initially full speed. If the firmware exposes a temperature source, choose Monitor CPU for RAD-FANS.
  2. The board supports BMC/IPMI access while the CPU is off with appropriate motherboard standby power. Use the completed power map and the dedicated IPMI port on a trusted local network. Do not power a loose board or attach an arbitrary secondary supply just to reach this interface.
  3. Photograph the actual control page and sensor names. The exact menu varies with firmware; do not follow raw IPMI fan commands copied from another board. If controls cannot be verified, keep CPU load testing pending. Do not flash firmware or clear CMOS as an automatic first troubleshooting step.
  4. At first host start, observe all three radiator fans turning. The splitter normally returns only one fan's tachometer signal: one RPM reading does not prove three functioning fans. Record which fan feeds that signal if marked.
  5. Check pump speed and CPU temperature. The original pump's published nominal speed is 4,000 ±10% RPM at 12 V. A zero, intermittent or markedly different reading requires review of sensor mapping and operation before load. A normal RPM reading alone does not prove coolant flow or correct cold-plate contact.

For a noise/temperature fault, stop the workload first. If the pump or required fans have failed or temperature continues rising, shut down the host using COMMISSIONING.md. Do not unplug a pump or fan while powered. Keep hands and tools out of the running radiator fans.

Optional existing temperature probes

ASRock lists two thermistor cables among its accessories; the user manual marks these optional, so ownership is unconfirmed. If present, T_SENSOR1 / T_SENSOR2 can support an inlet/nearby-air comparison after their readings are verified. Keep probe tips secured away from electrical contacts and fan blades. A probe attached to a heatsink reads that surface, not inlet air or GPU memory junction. Do not use a guessed replacement thermistor specification.
